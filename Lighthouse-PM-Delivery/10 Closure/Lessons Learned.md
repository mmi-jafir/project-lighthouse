# Lessons Learned
### Knowledge Infrastructure Workstream — Project Lighthouse

---

## 1. Legacy Content Quality Is Easy to Underestimate

The Wave 1 audit revealed metadata quality significantly below the original knowledge audit's estimate, triggering a mid-project budget decision (Decision 001). **Lesson:** when estimating migration effort for legacy content, sample a small subset in depth during the audit phase rather than relying on volume-based estimates alone — quality variance, not volume, was the real driver of the underestimate.

## 2. Regional Taxonomy Conflicts Are Predictable, Not Exceptional

The EMEA/APAC taxonomy disagreement (Decision 002) was, in hindsight, a foreseeable consequence of designing a single global taxonomy for a firm with genuinely distinct regional service lines. **Lesson:** for any global rollout, design the governance model to explicitly anticipate global-vs-regional tension (the federated approach) from the start, rather than discovering the need for it reactively.

## 3. A Strong Pilot Gate Prevents a Weak Launch

Enforcing the pilot performance threshold before the AI assistant's release (Decision 003) meant EMEA's actual go-live experience was solid, even though a flagship feature slipped. **Lesson:** decoupling a stable core deliverable from a still-maturing feature is often better than either delaying everything or launching everything together at reduced quality — this is a reusable pattern for any program with an experimental component alongside a stable one.

## 4. Automating PM Overhead Pays Off Quickly

The automations introduced mid-project (stale content alerts, change request routing, meeting action tracking) were each triggered by a specific pain point, not planned upfront. **Lesson:** rather than trying to automate everything in advance, watch for recurring manual friction in the first few months and automate reactively — this produced automations that were immediately useful rather than speculative.

## 5. AI Drafting Tools Speed Up PM Communication, But Judgment Still Matters

AI-assisted drafting (status reports, stakeholder emails, risk entries) consistently saved drafting time, but every example required a human correction of some kind — a tone adjustment, a factual specific, an ownership detail. **Lesson:** treat AI drafting as a first-pass accelerator, not a final output; the review step is where PM judgment remains essential and should not be skipped, even under time pressure.

## 6. Honest Communication About Known Limitations Builds Trust

The EMEA go-live communication explicitly acknowledged the AI assistant's delay and the reason for it, rather than minimizing the gap. Early feedback suggested this landed better than a more polished but evasive message would have. **Lesson:** for a sophisticated audience (in this case, consultants who are professionally skeptical of overclaiming), transparency about limitations is a credibility asset, not a liability.
