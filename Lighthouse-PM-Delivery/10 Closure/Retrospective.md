# Program Retrospective
### Knowledge Infrastructure Workstream — Project Lighthouse

**Format:** What Went Well / What Didn't / What We'll Carry Forward
**Participants (simulated):** Program Manager, Knowledge Governance Lead, Technology Lead, Regional Leads, Change Lead

---

## What Went Well

- Core platform and search functionality met or exceeded every target KPI at closure (search success 86% vs. 85% target; AI grounding 96% vs. 95% target)
- The wave-based regional migration approach allowed lessons from EMEA to directly improve the Americas and APAC waves, rather than repeating the same issues three times
- The decision log discipline (three formal decisions, each with documented options and rationale) meant no major program choice was made informally or went unrecorded
- Adoption exceeded target (74% vs. 70%) within three months, suggesting the phased AI launch and honest communication approach paid off
- PM process automations, though introduced reactively rather than planned upfront, meaningfully reduced manual overhead by the program's second half

## What Didn't Go Well

- The original migration effort estimate was too optimistic about legacy metadata quality, requiring a mid-project budget correction
- Content review completion finished slightly under target (88% vs. 90%), an area that will require continued attention post-closure rather than a clean handoff
- The AI assistant's Sprint 4 pilot shortfall, while handled well via Decision 003, could have been anticipated earlier if evaluation data had been sliced by document type/age from the start (see Lessons Learned)
- APAC adoption was still ramping at program closure, meaning the "70% within 3 months" target was met in aggregate but masked regional variation

## What We'll Carry Forward

- **Design for federated governance from day one** on any future global rollout, rather than defaulting to a single global standard and adjusting reactively
- **Slice pilot and evaluation data by relevant segments early** (region, document age, content type) to catch performance gaps faster
- **Treat automation as an ongoing practice, not a one-time setup** — the most useful automations in this program emerged from watching real friction during delivery, not from upfront planning
- **Preserve the pattern of decoupling stable deliverables from experimental ones** at launch — this protected the program's credibility during the AI assistant's slower ramp-up

## Closing Note

This program is one workstream within the broader Project Lighthouse initiative. Its outcomes and lessons — particularly around federated governance and phased feature rollout — are reflected back into the strategic and organizational design documented in the companion Transformation Strategy repository.
