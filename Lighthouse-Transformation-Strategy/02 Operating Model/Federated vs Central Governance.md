# Federated vs. Central Governance
### Project Lighthouse — Knowledge Infrastructure Transformation

**Version:** 1.0
**Related:** `../07 Decisions/001 Federated vs Central Governance.md` (formal decision record)

---

## 1. The Core Tension

A global professional services firm faces a structural tension in knowledge governance: **central control** ensures consistency and enables global search, but risks poor regional fit and low local adoption; **regional autonomy** ensures relevance and buy-in, but risks fragmenting the very knowledge base the transformation is meant to unify.

## 2. Why This Matters More for Solstice Advisory Than a Typical Enterprise

Unlike a single-market enterprise, Solstice Advisory's regions operate under different regulatory regimes, serve different industry mixes, and — critically — have historically operated with significant local autonomy in how they organize and share knowledge. A purely central model risks being perceived as a headquarters imposition; a purely regional model risks recreating the exact fragmentation the transformation exists to solve.

## 3. The Federated Model Adopted

Solstice Advisory adopts a **federated governance model**: a mandatory global standard (taxonomy spine, confidentiality classification, platform architecture) set centrally, with defined autonomy for regions and practices within that standard (regional taxonomy extensions, local adoption approaches, practice-specific content stewardship).

```text
GLOBAL (Mandatory)              REGIONAL / PRACTICE (Flexible)
─────────────────              ──────────────────────────────
Taxonomy spine                  Regional extension tags
Confidentiality classification  Local adoption campaigns
Platform architecture           Practice-specific content review cadence
Content lifecycle policy        Regional Knowledge Contact staffing model
```

## 4. Governing Principle

**Global for consistency and reuse; regional for relevance and adoption.** Any future policy question should be tested against this principle: does it require global consistency to preserve cross-regional search and reuse? If yes, it belongs centrally. If not, it belongs regionally.

## 5. Where This Was Tested in Practice

This model was directly exercised during delivery when EMEA and APAC raised conflicting taxonomy needs — the resulting resolution (a mandatory global spine plus regional extension layer) is documented in `Lighthouse-PM-Delivery/09 Decisions/002 Taxonomy Governance Approach.md` and validated the federated approach set out here.
