# Capability and Culture Plan
### Project Lighthouse — Knowledge Infrastructure Transformation

**Version:** 1.0
**Related:** `Incentive And Adoption Strategy.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

Beyond training people to use a new tool, this plan builds the firm's longer-term capability to sustain a knowledge-sharing culture — the difference between a successful launch and a lasting change in how the firm works.

## 2. Capability-Building Approach by Grade

| Grade | Capability Focus |
|---|---|
| Partners / Principals | Modeling knowledge-seeking and knowledge-sharing behavior visibly in client and internal settings |
| Managers / Consultants | Practical skill in writing effective case debriefs and classifying content correctly |
| Analysts / Associates | Embedding platform search and contribution habits at onboarding, before old habits (e.g., defaulting to personal folders) form |

## 3. Onboarding Integration

New hires are introduced to the platform as their **default knowledge tool from day one** of onboarding, rather than as an add-on after they've already developed workarounds. This specifically targets the attrition-driven knowledge loss identified in the Digital Maturity Assessment — capturing good habits early reduces the tacit-knowledge risk before it compounds over a consultant's tenure.

## 4. Champion Network

A Regional Adoption Champion is identified per office (typically a manager-level consultant with strong local credibility), providing peer-level support that scales better than a purely central help-desk model, and surfacing local friction points back to the Regional Knowledge Governance Contact.

## 5. Culture Signal: Leadership Behavior

Partners are asked to model two specific behaviors, chosen because they're visible and low-effort: citing the platform by name when referencing prior firm work in meetings, and prompting teams to complete a case debrief as a standard close-out step — normalizing the practice through visible repetition rather than mandate alone.

## 6. Long-Term Success Indicator

The clearest signal of genuine cultural change is not the adoption percentage at launch, but whether new consultants two years from now have never known a "pre-platform" way of working — i.e., the platform becomes invisible infrastructure rather than a change initiative people remember adopting.
