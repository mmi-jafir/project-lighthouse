# Digital Maturity Assessment
### Solstice Advisory — Knowledge & Digital Capability Baseline

**Version:** 1.0
**Prepared for:** Transformation Steering Committee
**Related:** `Transformation Strategy.md`, `Business Case.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

Before committing to a global knowledge infrastructure transformation, Solstice Advisory assessed its current digital and knowledge maturity across five dimensions, to ground the transformation strategy in an honest baseline rather than assumed best practice.

## 2. Maturity Dimensions and Findings

| Dimension | Current State | Maturity Level (1–5) |
|---|---|---:|
| **Technology** | Content spread across SharePoint, Confluence, shared drives, and email; no unified search; no AI-assisted retrieval | 2 — Fragmented |
| **Data & Content Governance** | Inconsistent ownership (42% of documents have an assigned owner); no firm-wide taxonomy; ad hoc classification by practice/region | 1 — Ad Hoc |
| **People & Culture** | Strong tacit knowledge-sharing within teams, but little firm-wide reuse culture; billable-hour incentives discourage documentation | 2 — Fragmented |
| **Process** | Case debriefs occur inconsistently; no structured capture of reusable assets post-engagement | 2 — Fragmented |
| **Leadership & Governance** | No firm-wide knowledge governance function; decisions made locally per office or practice | 2 — Fragmented |

**Maturity Scale:** 1 = Ad Hoc, 2 = Fragmented, 3 = Managed, 4 = Integrated, 5 = Optimized

## 3. Key Observations

- The firm's knowledge maturity is **below industry benchmark for a firm of its scale**, driven primarily by governance and incentive gaps rather than a lack of underlying content quality
- Regional and practice-level knowledge-sharing exists but does not scale globally — the firm effectively has many small, disconnected "pockets" of good practice
- The absence of a unifying taxonomy is the single largest blocker to any future AI/search capability, since AI retrieval quality depends heavily on consistent classification

## 4. Implication for Transformation Design

The assessment supports a transformation that prioritizes **governance and information architecture before technology deployment** — deploying a platform onto ungoverned content would simply digitize the existing fragmentation rather than resolve it. This directly shapes the phased approach in `Transformation Strategy.md`.
